# CD276 (B7-H3) target biology and modality assessment

**Scope:** lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC) only. No
NSCLC-wide or other-histology conclusions are drawn.

**Target:** CD276 / B7-H3, Ensembl ENSG00000103855.

**Sources:** configured MCP servers only (`target`, `drug`, `interaction`,
`pathway`, `expression`). No web search or scraping. Statements that are not
database output are explicitly labelled **PRIOR KNOWLEDGE**.

The database classifies CD276 unambiguously as a **surface antigen**. The
UniProt function annotation describes a type I membrane protein that "may
participate in the regulation of T-cell-mediated immune response", "may play a
protective role in tumour cells by inhibiting natural-killer mediated cell
lysis", and notes that isoforms 1 and 2 both modulate CD4 T-cell responses,
with isoform 2 enhancing cytotoxic T-cell induction and IFN-gamma production in
the presence of TCR signalling.

**PRIOR KNOWLEDGE:** B7-H3 is an immunoglobulin-superfamily B7-family
glycoprotein with a signal peptide, an extracellular IgV-IgC ectodomain, a
transmembrane helix and a short cytoplasmic tail. Human B7-H3 exists as a
2Ig (V-C) and a 4Ig (V-C-V-C) isoform generated by exon duplication; the 4Ig
form predominates in human tissues, whereas mouse predominantly expresses 2Ig.
No definitive receptor has been agreed, and the literature is divided on
whether B7-H3 is co-stimulatory or co-inhibitory. This contest is prior
knowledge, not a database annotation; the UniProt text itself emphasises an
NK-inhibitory, tumour-protective role.

**Homologues (Ensembl via Open Targets):** chimpanzee CD276 98.88% identity;
macaque 94.57%; pig 90.07%; guinea pig 89.70%; dog 93.63%; mouse Cd276 52.43%
query identity; rat 52.81%. The closest human paralogues are HHLA2 (12.92%)
and VTCN1/B7-H4 (11.99%); the wider BTN/BTNL family is 8.99-16.48%. The mouse
orthologue is only ~52% identical, and the guinea pig/dog/pig orthologues are
~90-94% identical. **Inference:** a human-selective B7-H3 antibody is unlikely
to be pharmacologically cross-reactive in mouse, whereas cynomolgus, dog and
pig are plausible toxicology species. This is a sequence-identity inference,
not a measured cross-reactivity result.

## 2. Localization, topology and what they imply

Subcellular localization returned:

- **Membrane** (UniProt)
- **Vesicles** (Human Protein Atlas)

The antibody-tractability criterion **"UniProt SigP or TMHMM" = TRUE** is
satisfied, i.e. the target carries a predicted signal peptide and/or
transmembrane segment. The SignalP xref is "SignalP-noTM", which on its own
predicts a secretory signal peptide rather than a transmembrane helix; the
combined UniProt/GO annotation nevertheless places the protein at the membrane.
The HPA "Vesicles" annotation is consistent with a surface protein that
traffics through endosomal compartments, but it is a localization class, not a
measurement of endocytosis.

**Implication:** a cell-surface, extracellularly exposed ectodomain is directly
accessible to antibodies and antibody derivatives. This is the structural basis
for the antibody/ADC/engager modalities. It also removes small-molecule and
PROTAC options that require an intracellular binding site and E3-ligase
access, unless a cell-permeable binder to an intracellular surface exists -
which the databases do not provide.

Reading these data:

1. **CD276 is broadly expressed at low-to-moderate RNA level in normal tissue,
   not tumour-restricted.** Roughly two-thirds of sampled normal tissues are at
   level >= 3. This is the central off-tumour-risk fact for cytotoxic
   modalities.
2. **Lung itself is a moderate-to-high normal expresser** (1,683 TPM, level 4;
   HPA protein level 2 with reliability). This matters specifically for LUAD
   and SCLC: the target is present in the organ being treated, so the
   therapeutic window depends on tumour-versus-normal protein density and
   accessibility, not on binary presence/absence.
3. **Bronchus shows an RNA/protein discordance** (RNA not detected, HPA protein
   level 2, reliable). This may reflect airway epithelial protein expression
   not captured by bulk RNA or antibody sensitivity; it should be treated as a
   potential on-target airway toxicity signal rather than dismissed.
4. **Immune myeloid cells are the highest expressers** (macrophages 9,456-14,497
   TPM), while lymphoid cells are low (3-19 TPM). This means myeloid-rich
   tissue and the tumour microenvironment will present abundant target.
5. **Brain bulk RNA is moderate-high (1,538.5 TPM, level 4)** but this is bulk
   tissue RNA and does not establish protein at the blood-brain barrier or on
   neurons.
6. The expression MCP comparison endpoint reports a **57-entry subset** (median
   988.5 TPM, mean 1,664.0 TPM, max 14,497 TPM). The full 120-entry table gives
   the median 834 TPM quoted above. Both are descriptive of the same dataset at
   different scopes.

## 5. Modality-relevant biology

### 5.1 Surface exposure

Strong. ChEMBL surface-antigen class, UniProt membrane localization, and a
positive signal-peptide/TM criterion all agree. The entire ectodomain is
extracellular. This supports antibody, ADC, bispecific/engager and CAR-based
approaches.

### 5.2 Internalization (ADC requirement)

**Not established by these databases.** No internalization rate, recycling
kinetics, or payload-delivery data are returned. The HPA "Vesicles" annotation
is only compatible with endosomal trafficking.

**PRIOR KNOWLEDGE:** B7-H3 is internalized on antibody binding and is used as an
ADC target; this is the mechanistic premise for the clinical-stage B7-H3 ADCs.
The absolute internalization rate, antigen turnover and the fraction of surface
antigen that reaches lysosomes are target- and cell-line-dependent and must be
measured experimentally (e.g. surface-binding/internalization assays in LUAD and
SCLC models) before ADC payload chemistry is chosen.

### 5.3 T-cell engagement / bispecific suitability

Structurally suitable: a surface antigen with an accessible ectodomain.
However, the broad normal expression described above is a direct liability for
T-cell engagers, because even low-density normal-tissue antigen can trigger
cytotoxicity when a high-avidity T-cell engager is used. Antigen density per
cell is **not retrievable** here; HPA protein levels are ordinal IHC scores, not
molecules per cell. A T-cell engager programme would need a tumour-selectivity
argument (epitope, isoform, density threshold or masked/prodrug design) that
these data do not provide.

### 5.4 Antigen density and modulation

**Not established.** The databases give tissue-level RNA (TPM) and ordinal
protein levels; they do not give surface molecules per cell, receptor occupancy,
or antigen modulation after antibody binding. Broad normal expression plus
high macrophage expression implies a large soluble/cellular antigen sink, but
the magnitude is not in these data.

### 5.5 Isoforms and shedding

The database returned 19 transcript IDs and the synonym "4Ig-B7-H3", and the
UniProt function text explicitly refers to isoforms 1 and 2. No isoform-resolved
expression or protein abundance is available.

**PRIOR KNOWLEDGE:** the 2Ig and 4Ig isoforms differ in the number of
extracellular Ig domains and can differ in function; human tumours
predominantly express 4Ig-B7-H3. The ectodomain can be proteolytically shed to
yield soluble B7-H3 (sB7-H3) in plasma, which may act as a decoy/sink for
antibodies and ADCs and as a circulating biomarker. Neither the shedding rate
nor sB7-H3 levels are retrievable from these MCP servers.

### 5.6 Blood-brain-barrier relevance for SCLC

SCLC frequently metastasizes to the brain. The databases show moderate bulk
brain CD276 RNA (1,538.5 TPM, level 4) but provide no protein-level or
blood-brain-barrier data. The drug record for 8H9 131I includes a terminated
phase 2 trial in **leptomeningeal metastasis** and a phase 1 record in DIPG,
which demonstrates that B7-H3 in the CNS compartment has been targeted, but by
compartmental/intrathecal or locoregional delivery, not by systemic
administration. **No conclusion can be drawn about systemic antibody or ADC
brain penetration from these data.**

## 6. Modality assessment

| Modality | Assessment | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Small molecule** | **Not viable** | No pocket, no ligand, no structure-with-ligand, no probe, no SM tractability flag; the druggable surface is a large Ig-domain protein-protein interface. |
| **Bare antibody** | **Highly suitable** (binding/effector) | Surface antigen with a positive advanced-clinical tractability flag; two clinical-stage antibodies (enoblituzumab, 8H9 131I). Key choices are epitope, isoform selectivity and effector function. The co-stimulatory versus co-inhibitory function is contested, so an agonist/antagonist rationale cannot be assumed from the database. |
| **Antibody-drug conjugate** | **Highly suitable in principle** | Surface antigen with an extracellular ectodomain and clinical precedent for B7-H3 ADCs (prior knowledge). Requires experimental confirmation of internalization and of tumour-versus-normal antigen density; broad normal expression and macrophage-rich sink raise off-tumour payload risk. |
| **T-cell engager / bispecific** | **Suitable but narrow therapeutic window** | Surface antigen is accessible, but broad low-level normal expression and unquantified antigen density make on-target/off-tumour toxicity the central risk. |
| **Degrader / PROTAC** | **Not viable** | Cell-surface/extracellular target, no small-molecule binder, no ubiquitination-site evidence; the "database ubiquitination" and "half-life data" flags are generic and do not enable degradation. |
| **CAR-T / cell therapy** | Feasible on surface-exposure grounds, but same on-target/off-tumour concern | Not directly assessed by the tractability output; listed for completeness. |

**Most promising modality:** antibody-based. The strongest database support is
for a surface-antigen antibody, and the strongest plausible therapeutic index
argument is an antibody-drug conjugate or an engineered antibody format that
exploits tumour-associated antigen density rather than a signalling agonist or
antagonist mechanism. Small molecule and PROTAC routes should be deprioritised.

## 7. What the databases do NOT establish

1. **Tumour expression in LUAD or SCLC.** The expression MCP contains only
   normal tissue (123 entries, no tumour samples). It cannot show whether CD276
   is overexpressed in LUAD or SCLC relative to normal lung; that requires the
   single-cell/genomics workstreams or tumour IHC.
2. **Surface protein density per cell** in tumour or normal tissue.
3. **Internalization rate, kinetics, recycling or lysosomal delivery.**
4. **Shedding rate or soluble B7-H3 concentration.**
5. **Isoform-resolved expression** (2Ig versus 4Ig) in any tissue.
6. **The functional receptor and signalling direction.** No Reactome pathway is
   annotated, the IntAct interactions are low-confidence (scores 0.27-0.53), and
   the co-stimulatory/co-inhibitory question is unresolved in the literature.
7. **Blood-brain-barrier permeability** for systemic antibodies or ADCs.
8. **Therapeutic index or off-tumour toxicity** in LUAD/SCLC; this requires the
   safety and clinical workstreams.
9. **Whether an antibody cross-reacts with mouse B7-H3**; the 52% sequence
   identity predicts it will not, but this is an inference from identity.
